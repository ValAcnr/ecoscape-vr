# EcoScape VR — Partners summary

*All organisations checked on 1 November 2026: website live and recent activity confirmed where possible. Full list with sources: `partners.xlsx` (sheets **NYC** and **Paris-France**, also as CSV).*

## How we picked them

Each partner fills one of four roles in the Earn-to-Plant model:

| Role | What we need |
|---|---|
| **Planter** | Physically plants and maintains the unlocked plantings |
| **Sponsor** | Funds plantings in exchange for visibility and impact reporting |
| **Data** | Provides trees, heat and permit data for the map |
| **Visibility** | Networks, labels and events that bring players and credibility |

## Top picks — Paris / France

1. **City of Paris — *permis de végétaliser*** (legal framework). A legal version of guerrilla gardening already exists: groups plant on public space under a charter (local and bee-friendly plants, no pesticides, year-round care). There are 1,732 permits in open data. EcoScape can be its digital front door.
2. **La SAUGE** and **Halage** (planters). La SAUGE runs or supports six urban farms in the Paris region and already sells corporate solidarity days. Halage grows local flowers through a work-integration scheme (Fleurs d'Halage), which adds a strong social-impact story.
3. **botanic®** (sponsor and plant supply). This garden retailer removed synthetic pesticides in 2008 and has been a *société à mission* since 2021, which fits the charter's no-pesticide rule.
4. **Parisculteurs** (sites) for edible plantings on rooftops and schoolyards.
5. **UNAF** (pollinator expertise). Important design lesson: UNAF reports that 90% of surveyed beekeepers oppose GPS location of hives. **The app must never show hive locations.**

**Equivalents of Stavian.** Stavian is a Vietnamese industrial group (plastics and petrochemicals) whose visible CSR work focuses on education and health. We found no Stavian tree-planting programme. The closest French equivalents are industrial groups with **public biodiversity commitments under act4nature international**:

| Sector | Signatories |
|---|---|
| Construction & materials | VINCI, Saint-Gobain, Bouygues Construction |
| Energy | EDF, ENGIE |
| Retail | Kingfisher (Castorama), Carrefour |

**Gap:** no paper or packaging company appears among the act4nature signatories. A paper-sector equivalent is still `[À VÉRIFIER]`.

**Benchmarks, not partners:** Reforest'Action and Plantons pour l'Avenir show that French companies already pay for tree planting, mostly in forests outside cities. Our difference is urban, educational and pollinator/edible.

## Top picks — New York

1. **Forest for All NYC.** A coalition of 200+ organisations convened by The Nature Conservancy and already working on the Local Law 148 Urban Forest Plan. One contact opens many doors.
2. **Trees New York.** It trains licensed "Citizen Pruners" and runs school plantings, and it has a corporate sponsorship page. It matches our "learn to maintain" module.
3. **New York Restoration Project.** It gave away 3,750 free trees in spring 2026 and over 80,500 since 2008, and it is supported by **Bloomberg Philanthropies**. NYRP is therefore the most realistic route to Bloomberg.
4. **NYC Parks** (Forestry, GreenThumb). It is the authority for street trees and supports 550+ community gardens, which suits the edible/pollinator option. Caution: an October 2026 Council op-ed says Parks no longer takes individual street-tree requests.
5. **BetaNYC.** Civic tech and open data. Its NYC School of Data 2027 call for proposals closes **6 December 2026**, a possible stage for the prototype.

**NYCEDC** is less direct: its BATWorks climate hub opens in 2028, and the "Pilots at BAT" programme is the only near-term entry point.

**Warning:** the MillionTreesNYC programme was completed in 2015, and its old domain now redirects to an unrelated website. Do not cite or link it.
